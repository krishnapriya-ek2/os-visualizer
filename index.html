Core Mathematical Formulas

CPU Scheduling Metrics
  • Turnaround Time (TAT) = Completion Time (CT) - Arrival Time (AT)
  • Waiting Time (WT) = Turnaround Time (TAT) - Burst Time (BT)
  • Response Time (RT) = First CPU Allocation Time - Arrival Time (AT)
  • Average WT = (∑ WT) / Number of Processes
  • Average TAT = (∑ TAT) / Number of Processes
  • Throughput = Total Completed Processes / Total Time Span
Disk Scheduling Metrics
  • Seek Distance between tracks A and B = | A - B |
  • Total Head Movement = ∑ | Cylinder_{i} - Cylinder_{i-1} |
  • Average Seek Distance = Total Head Movement / Total Serviced Requests

Algorithms Quick Reference

Algorithm Type Starvation Risk? Time Complexity Key Characteristics
FCFS (Disk) Fair No O(N) Simple, zero overhead, high seek latency.
SSTF Greedy Yes (distant tracks) O(N²) Minimizes immediate seek; may cause starvation.
SCAN Elevator No O(N log N) Sweeps to disk boundary, then reverses.
C-SCAN Circular No O(N log N) Sweeps in one direction only; uniform wait times.
LOOK Optimized No O(N log N) Reverses at highest/lowest request; avoids boundary waste.
SJF (CPU) Greedy Yes (long jobs) O(N²) Optimal minimum average waiting time.
Round Robin Preemptive No O(N) per slice Time-shared fair queue; heavily dependent on quantum Q.

Common MCA Viva Voce Questions & Model Answers

Q1: Why is SSTF prone to starvation?
In SSTF (Shortest Seek Time First), the head prioritizes whichever pending cylinder request is closest to its current position. If new requests continuously arrive near the current head position, requests far away on the outer or inner tracks will be postponed indefinitely, leading to starvation.
Q2: How does LOOK improve upon the classic SCAN algorithm?
Classic SCAN (Elevator algorithm) always continues traveling until it strikes the physical end of the disk (track 0 or disk_size - 1) before reversing direction, even if no pending requests exist beyond the current head. LOOK checks ahead and reverses immediately at the furthest requested cylinder, avoiding useless seek travel to the physical boundary.
Q3: How does the choice of Time Quantum (Q) impact Round Robin scheduling?
If the Time Quantum is extremely large, Round Robin degenerates into FCFS (First-Come First-Served). If the Time Quantum is extremely small, CPU context-switching overhead becomes dominant, crippling throughput. The optimal rule of thumb is setting Q such that roughly 80% of CPU bursts are shorter than Q.
Q4: Why does SJF provide the minimum average waiting time?
By executing the shortest job first, short processes finish rapidly and leave the system, significantly reducing the cumulative waiting time experienced by all subsequent processes. This property is mathematically proven using the rearrangement inequality.
